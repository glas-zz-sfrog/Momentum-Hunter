# ARGUS-STRATEGY-SCIENCE-LAB-001 — Research Evidence Matrix

**Status:** Research and architecture evidence only
**As of:** 2026-08-29
**Production authority:** None. This document does not change candidate generation, scoring, TradePlan behavior, sizing, risk, exits, execution, Paper, Shadow, or live-order authority.

## Executive conclusion

Argus should begin with auditable probabilistic and distributional baselines, not an end-to-end trade oracle. The first model ladder is regularized linear/logistic models, matched-event and historical-analog estimates, hierarchical or empirical-Bayes partial pooling, generalized additive models, quantile/survival models, and transparent graph diffusion. Gradient-boosted, ranking, deeper temporal, language, graph-neural, bandit, causal-ML, agentic, foundation-model, and reinforcement-learning methods receive authority only after the simpler ladder exposes a defined deficiency and the complex method clears the same untouched temporal evaluation.

The literature supports these methods as tools; it does **not** establish that any one of them produces executable Argus alpha. Published backtests are treated as hypotheses, not performance evidence. Every GO below means “admit to a bounded, read-only research comparison after its data gate,” never “promote to production.”

## Reading rules and decision vocabulary

- **Unit of analysis:** independence is reported through named prediction units, primary clusters, and secondary/multiway clusters—not a scalar effective-sample estimate. Overlapping horizons, repeated issuer events, securities sharing one source catalyst, and same-day market/sector shocks do not count as separate independent evidence.
- **Ranges:** The sample-size ranges are conservative Argus planning heuristics, not universal statistical thresholds. Final power depends on class balance, censoring, effect size, dependence, feature count, and regime coverage. Every experiment must report raw rows, each named cluster count, largest-cluster concentration, overlap, and cluster/block or multiway-cluster intervals.
- **Temporal integrity:** “Feasible” means the method can run behind a fail-closed Point-in-Time Data Gateway with rolling/expanding training, embargoed labels, frozen feature definitions, and historical-universe reconstruction. It does not mean the current data already satisfy those conditions.
- **GO:** method is justified for its stated research role once prerequisite PIT data exist. **NEEDS-DATA:** intellectually plausible but the current evidence/data contract is insufficient. **NO-GO:** do not admit under this directive; a new directive and new evidence would be required.
- **Roadmap classes:** `BASELINE` is a mandatory comparator; `CHAMPION_CANDIDATE` may compete for a research champion role; `CHALLENGER` tests incremental value; `SHADOW_ONLY` may create frozen evidence but has no decision authority; `DEFERRED` waits for a named deficiency/data gate; `REJECTED` is out of the present ladder.
- **Missingness:** absence is represented by an explicit reason code and availability timestamp. No model may infer that a later-observed value was “missing” at an earlier cutoff unless that missing state itself is point-in-time reproducible.
- **Common evaluation:** all families face the same admitted periods, universe, costs, execution model, event routing, candidate set, and frozen search ledger. No reported paper result is imported as an Argus performance expectation.

## Evidence matrix A — prediction, mechanism, data, and missingness

| # | Model family | Intended prediction object and hypothesized mechanism | Point-in-time data and independent-cluster planning floor | Missing-data behavior |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regularized linear regression | Conditional mean reaction, gap, MFE/MAE, or executable return. Shrinkage may recover a stable low-dimensional linear signal from many noisy, correlated predictors. | Versioned numeric features and continuous labels. Start around **500–2,000 independent event-date/issuer clusters** for a tightly regularized sparse model; larger when features or event classes expand. | Requires explicit imputation plus missing indicators or models fit on defined complete subsets. Never median-fill across the future or across a later universe. |
| 2 | Logistic regression | Event success, positive reaction, target-before-loss, loss-before-target, or abstention-worthy probability. Mechanism is additive log-odds with auditable coefficients. | Binary labels, PIT features, and class-balanced temporal folds. Plan for **300–1,000 independent labeled events** and at least roughly **30–50 minority outcomes per retained degree of freedom** after shrinkage; rare separation requires penalization. | Same explicit imputation/indicator rule as linear models. Complete-case analysis is acceptable only if missingness is demonstrably unrelated to the label conditional on retained inputs. |
| 3 | Generalized additive models (GAMs) | Probabilities or means with smooth univariate nonlinearities: momentum saturation, liquidity thresholds, option-implied-move curvature, or cash-runway effects without opaque interactions. | PIT tabular features and **1,000–5,000 clusters** for a modest set of penalized smooths; interactions require materially more. | Can include a missing category/indicator and a separate smooth only where support exists. Extrapolation beyond observed support must trigger OOD/abstention. |
| 4 | Hierarchical models | Event-class, sector, issuer, or relationship-specific probabilities/distributions with partial pooling. Mechanism: related groups share information while retaining heterogeneity. | Explicit hierarchy, group identities valid at cutoff, and **1,000+ total clusters**, usually **100–300 per major event class**; thin groups may borrow strength but do not acquire imaginary information. | Missing group-level covariates may be modeled probabilistically; unknown group identity remains explicit. Priors and pooling structure are versioned and sensitivity-tested. |
| 5 | Empirical-Bayes partial pooling | Shrunk event/issuer/sector rates, edge strengths, or calibration offsets. Mechanism: noisy cell estimates are pulled toward an estimated population distribution. | Exchangeability partition fixed ex ante; about **1,000+ named primary event clusters** and **50–200 observations per estimable cell** is a useful starting range. Report independent cell/event counts separately from quote rows. | Natural for sparse cells, but a cell with no admissible data receives a labeled population estimate, not a fabricated observation. Hyperparameter estimation uses training history only. |
| 6 | Gradient-boosted trees | Nonlinear mean/probability/ranking response where interactions among event, positioning, liquidity, and price state may matter. | PIT tabular corpus, immutable category encodings, and usually **5,000–20,000 named primary clusters** for stable tuning; raw row counts can be orders of magnitude larger without adding primary-cluster evidence. | Native missing branches can be useful, but missingness routing itself can leak provider/vintage artifacts. Compare with explicit indicators and missing-feature ablations. |
| 7 | Smooth boosted trees | Smooth nonlinear means/probabilities with controlled interaction structure. Mechanism: retain boosting flexibility while reducing discontinuous threshold instability and improving effect inspection. | Same inputs as GBT; plan for **2,000–10,000 clusters** for shallow smooth/additive structures, more for interactions. | Must define how missing values enter smooth gates; unknown values should not be silently mapped onto a learned numeric region. |
| 8 | Learning-to-rank methods | Within-cutoff ordering of candidates by future executable utility or reaction. Mechanism: optimize pairwise/listwise ordering rather than whole-universe point accuracy. | Dated candidate lists with common opportunity sets, PIT features, outcomes, and costs. Need roughly **2,000–10,000 independent lists/event dates**; generated pairs are not independent sample growth. | Candidates with incomplete evidence may receive explicit coverage features or be abstained. Training must not drop hard missing cases in a way unavailable online. |
| 9 | Quantile regression | Conditional gap, return, MFE, MAE, or slippage quantiles. Mechanism: estimate asymmetric tails directly rather than infer them from a mean and Gaussian residual. | Continuous outcome with enough tail support; typically **2,000–10,000 clusters**, with each event class retaining meaningful observations near requested quantiles. | Quantile models still require explicit feature missingness. Censored/truncated outcomes require censor-aware variants; missing tails cannot be repaired by imputation. |
| 10 | Distributional regression | Full conditional reaction distribution or interpretable location/scale/shape parameters. Mechanism: features move volatility, skew, and tail shape separately. | Rich continuous outcomes and usually **5,000–20,000 clusters**; complex families demand more tail events and diagnostic coverage. | Likelihood must support censoring/truncation where present. Missing covariates use explicit policies; an unsupported distribution family must fail validation rather than force a fit. |
| 11 | Expected-shortfall models | Conditional VaR/expected shortfall for gap loss, skipped-stop loss, or action loss. Mechanism: directly model the severity of the bad tail relevant to capital risk. | Clean loss distribution, tail-complete execution outcomes, and normally **5,000–25,000 clusters**, including at least **hundreds of realized tail observations** at the chosen level. | Missing extreme outcomes are fatal selection bias. Halt, delisting, stale quote, and unfilled-order states must remain in the loss distribution, not be imputed away. |
| 12 | Survival analysis | Time to target, loss, confirmation, resolution, or timeout with right censoring. Mechanism: hazards use incomplete follow-up without pretending every path resolves. | Start/stop rules, censor reason, time-varying covariates known at each time, and **1,000–5,000 clusters** with roughly **300+ observed endpoints** for a modest model. | Principled for right censoring if censoring assumptions are defensible. Informative loss of coverage, delisting, or halt needs explicit modeling/sensitivity analysis. |
| 13 | Competing-risk models | Cumulative incidence/time for mutually exclusive first outcomes: target, loss, timeout, cancellation, halt, dilution, or event delay. | Complete outcome typing and censoring; typically **2,000–10,000 clusters** with about **200+ events of each modeled cause** for stable simple models. | Handles mutually exclusive observed causes; unknown cause is not censoring by default. Missing outcome type requires an explicit unresolved category or exclusion sensitivity. |
| 14 | Historical nearest-neighbor / analog methods | Conditional empirical outcome distribution from genuinely similar prior events. Mechanism: local recurrence without assuming a global function. | Standardized PIT features, a frozen distance metric, complete historical universe, and usually **1,000+ total events** with **10–30 independent close analogs per query**. | Distance must penalize/partition missing dimensions; “nearest” across incomparable evidence coverage is invalid. Return no estimate when neighborhood support is weak. |
| 15 | Transparent graph diffusion | Weighted read-through from a source event to peers, customers, suppliers, partners, sectors, or ETFs. Mechanism: economically linked targets may incorporate source news slowly. | Time-valid edges with type/source/confidence plus source shocks and target reactions. Need roughly **2,000+ independent source-event→target clusters**, hundreds of recurring edges, and enough edge turnover to test staleness. | Missing edge means “unknown,” not “no relationship.” Unknown source materiality/edge validity reduces weight or forces abstention. |
| 16 | Hawkes / temporal event-propagation models | Conditional intensity and timing of follow-on news, price jumps, volume bursts, or cross-entity events. Mechanism: self-/cross-excitation captures decaying event propagation. | Precisely timestamped events with known observation windows. Usually **5,000+ timestamped events per routed class**; repeated micro-events from one catalyst remain dependent. | Observation gaps and feed outages alter intensity and must enter the likelihood/exposure window. Imprecise timestamps are intervals, not fabricated instants. |
| 17 | Graph neural networks (GNNs) | Node-level reaction/ranking/distribution using learned aggregation over a dynamic economic graph. Mechanism: nonlinear multihop interactions may add information beyond explicit diffusion. | PIT graph snapshots, edge provenance, node features, labels, negative controls, and typically **20,000–100,000 node-time outcomes** plus thousands of validated edges across many independent event dates. | Requires masks and missingness features, but message passing can spread one provider artifact widely. Unknown/stale edges must be masked and ablated. |
| 18 | FinBERT / financial language encoders | Document/sentence class, sentiment, uncertainty, guidance change, contradiction, event type, or extracted span—not a trade. Mechanism: finance-domain contextual embeddings improve structured text tasks. | Timestamped source text, document version, tokenizer/model identity, and human gold labels. Plan for **2,000–10,000 labeled sentences/documents**, with **200–500 examples per rare label** or careful few-shot evaluation. | Missing document is explicit. Truncation, OCR loss, table omission, and unsupported language are coverage failures. Never backfill with a later amended transcript. |
| 19 | Retrieval-grounded LLM extraction | Structured claims with citation spans: facts, guidance, dates/windows, relationships, contradictions, and uncertainty. Mechanism: retrieval constrains generation to frozen evidence and exposes provenance. | Immutable cutoff corpus, retrieval index identity, prompt/model snapshot, and **1,000–5,000 adjudicated claims across 500–2,000 documents** for an initial extraction suite. | Must output `UNAVAILABLE/CONFLICTING` and cite evidence. Retrieval miss and absence of evidence remain distinct; generation may not fill either from parametric memory. |
| 20 | Autonomous research agents | Prospectively frozen research packets, hypotheses, evidence coverage, and structured scores. Mechanism: tool orchestration may discover heterogeneous evidence a fixed pipeline misses. | Full action/tool trace, retrieved artifacts, prompts/models, costs, and adjudicated prospective outcomes. At least **500–2,000 independent frozen cases** before even a narrow incremental claim; likely more because agents and prompts drift. | Agents must surface failed tools, unavailable sources, contradictions, and coverage. Silent substitution or narrative completion is failure. |
| 21 | Time-series foundation models | Zero-/few-shot probabilistic forecasts of price, volume, spread, volatility, or derived series. Mechanism: large cross-domain pretraining may transfer generic temporal motifs. | Exact checkpoint/pretraining disclosure, univariate/multivariate series, and finance-specific untouched evaluation. External pretraining does **not** add local primary-cluster evidence; require at least **2,000+ nonoverlapping local forecast origins** across regimes and report issuer/date clusters separately. | Often tolerate masks/padding, but semantics vary by checkpoint. Unknown pretraining overlap and revised-series contamination are first-order data risks. |
| 22 | Deep temporal models | Multi-horizon conditional distributions from sequential prices, volume, event state, and covariates. Mechanism: recurrent/attention architectures may capture nonlinear state and horizon interactions. | Dense synchronized sequences with availability masks and **20,000+ sequences drawn from at least ~2,000 independent days/events**; overlapping windows do not count independently. | Mask explicitly; distinguish not-yet-published, market-closed, feed outage, and genuine zero. Padding/mask leakage is a required negative control. |
| 23 | Conformal prediction and calibration | Prediction sets/intervals or abstention thresholds around any base probability, quantile, or score. Mechanism: calibrate residual/nonconformity ranks without specifying a full model. | Frozen base predictions and a temporally later calibration set. Roughly **500–2,000 independent calibration outcomes** for broad marginal coverage; many thousands for sector/event/tail-conditional claims. | A base predictor may handle missingness, but calibration strata with weak support must widen/abstain. Coverage is not universal under arbitrary dependence or shift. |
| 24 | Online calibration methods | Sequentially adjusted probability/interval coverage under drift. Mechanism: update a calibration parameter from revealed past errors without retraining the base model. | Ordered prediction/outcome pairs, delay map, and immutable pre-outcome predictions. The prediction unit is one frozen forecast origin; the primary cluster is event date and issuer is a secondary cluster. Require **1,000+ sequential outcomes** and **100+ unique event-date primary clusters per reported regime/window** for diagnostic usefulness. | Delayed/unrevealed labels remain pending; updates may use only revealed outcomes. Missing-label processes can bias adaptation and require sensitivity checks. |
| 25 | Contextual bandits | Action policy among IGNORE/WATCH/SCOUT/ENTER/CONFIRM/REDUCE based on context and partial feedback. Mechanism: balance information acquisition and utility. | Logged action propensities, contexts, feasible actions, outcomes, and costs. Credible offline evaluation commonly needs **10,000–100,000+ independent decisions** with nontrivial support for each action; Argus does not yet have randomized exploration data. | Only chosen-action reward is observed. Missing counterfactuals are structural, not imputable; absent propensities defeat reliable off-policy evaluation. |
| 26 | Supervised policy learning | Choose an action maximizing estimated utility from logged observational data. Mechanism: estimate action-specific outcomes/advantages, then apply explicit constraints. | PIT contexts, all feasible actions, logged behavior, action/outcome definitions, costs, and overlap. Plan for **2,000–10,000 independent decisions**, with adequate support for every action-context region. | Counterfactual outcomes remain missing and require identification assumptions. No-support regions force baseline action/abstention, not extrapolation. |
| 27 | Offline reinforcement learning | Long-horizon scout/confirm/scale/exit policy from fixed trajectories. Mechanism: optimize cumulative reward while accounting for sequential state/action effects. | Markov-enough state, behavior propensities, rewards, transitions, terminal outcomes, and broad action coverage. Credible deep offline RL usually needs **100,000+ transitions across many independent episodes**; current Argus evidence is nowhere near this requirement. | Counterfactual actions are unobserved; missing state variables create confounding and non-Markov behavior. No algorithm can repair absent coverage of consequential actions. |
| 28 | Causal forests | Conditional average effect of a defined treatment/exposure, such as scout vs no scout or source-event exposure, across covariates. Mechanism: honest forests estimate heterogeneous treatment effects. | Explicit treatment, outcome, confounders measured before treatment, and overlap. Typically **5,000–20,000 independent units**, including **500+ treated/control units in relevant broad groups**; leaf-level support still governs. | Handles covariate missingness only with explicit policy. Missing confounders or treatment-dependent missingness invalidate causal interpretation; weak overlap forces trimming. |
| 29 | Double machine learning (DML) | Low-dimensional average/conditional treatment or structural effect with flexible nuisance models. Mechanism: orthogonal scores plus cross-fitting reduce regularization bias. | Pre-specified estimand, treatment, outcome, confounders/instruments, cluster structure, and **2,000–10,000 independent observations** with overlap; high-dimensional nuisance complexity raises requirements. | Nuisance models may use imputation/masks, but causal identification still fails for missing confounders, invalid instruments, or selection on future availability. |
| 30 | Matched-event / causal-event-study methods | Abnormal reaction or incremental effect around an event relative to matched/control securities or synthetic counterfactuals. Mechanism: isolate event-specific movement from market/sector/common shocks. | PIT event timestamps, historical membership, returns, matching covariates, control pool, and pretrend window. Start near **500 events overall** and **100–300 per routed class**, with match quality more important than row volume. | Missing controls or poor common support trigger no match. Delistings, halts, and missing terminal prices remain outcomes; post-event matching variables are prohibited. |

## Evidence matrix B — interpretability, calibration, chronology, and bias risk

| # | Model family | Interpretability and calibration capability | Temporal-integrity feasibility | Leakage and survivorship risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regularized linear regression | **High.** Signed standardized coefficients and partial predictions are exact. Gaussian intervals require diagnostics; conformal or isotonic calibration can wrap outputs. | **High.** Deterministic fit/inference; rolling feature normalization and penalty selection must be training-only. | **Medium.** Imputation, standardization, feature selection, revised fundamentals, and overlapping forward labels are common leaks; survivor-only universes bias coefficients. |
| 2 | Logistic regression | **High.** Log-odds contributions are exact. Probabilities support Platt/isotonic/beta or online calibration; use reliability curves and Brier/log loss. | **High.** Time-split fit and calibration are straightforward. | **Medium.** Outcome-balanced sampling needs probability correction; separation and label-window overlap can exaggerate certainty; failed/delisted names must stay in the denominator. |
| 3 | GAMs | **High.** Shape functions reveal nonlinear effects; pairwise terms remain inspectable. Probabilities/distributions can be calibrated normally. | **High.** Smooth selection and knot/support choices must be learned inside each training fold. | **Medium.** Global smoothing and support boundaries can see future distributions; survivor filtering makes shapes too benign. |
| 4 | Hierarchical models | **High–medium.** Group effects and posterior uncertainty are explicit, but prior/pooling choices matter. Natural posterior predictive calibration plus external checks. | **High** if priors and group mappings are frozen per origin; expensive rolling refits are manageable offline. | **Medium.** Future group composition, taxonomy revisions, or hyperparameters estimated from full history leak. Partial pooling can conceal survivor selection across thin groups. |
| 5 | Empirical-Bayes partial pooling | **High.** Shrinkage target and cell estimates are explicit. Predictive intervals require hyperparameter uncertainty or bootstrap. | **High.** Estimate hyperparameters only from the available past and record population definition. | **Medium.** Full-period shrinkage targets leak; selective cells and disappearing issuers distort the population prior. |
| 6 | Gradient-boosted trees | **Medium.** Exact tree path, PDP/ALE/SHAP are descriptive, not causal. Raw scores often need post-hoc probability/quantile calibration. | **High** computationally, but every preprocessing/tuning stage must be nested in temporal folds. | **High.** Trees exploit timestamp, provider, missingness, universe, and target-construction artifacts aggressively; dead/delisted securities and failed event records are essential. |
| 7 | Smooth boosted trees | **Medium–high.** Smooth main effects/gates are more stable to inspect than hard splits. Calibration still must be measured. | **High** with versioned smoothing/tuning inside the training window. | **Medium–high.** Smoother surfaces reduce threshold brittleness but do not cure future normalization, provider, or survivor leakage. |
| 8 | Learning-to-rank | **Medium.** Ranking contributions can be inspected for tree/linear rankers, but scores are not probabilities. Separate utility and probability calibration is mandatory. | **High** only if each list is the actual contemporaneous eligible set and listwise folds are by time. | **High.** Future universe membership, cross-list pair construction, outcome-based candidate inclusion, and pair-count pseudo-replication are acute. |
| 9 | Quantile regression | **High–medium.** Coefficients/shapes by quantile are auditable. Quantile crossing and empirical coverage require correction. | **High.** Fit, crossing repair, and calibration use past data only. | **Medium.** Winsorization with full-sample cutoffs, omitted halts/gaps, and survivor-only tails understate risk. |
| 10 | Distributional regression | **Medium–high.** Named location/scale/shape effects are interpretable when the family is defensible. PIT/CRPS and calibration plots test output. | **High** for classical distributional models; family selection must be nested. | **Medium–high.** Choosing distributions after seeing test tails and excluding unpriceable outcomes are major risks. |
| 11 | Expected-shortfall models | **Medium.** VaR/ES is decision-relevant but tail-driver attribution varies. Joint VaR–ES scoring enables coherent comparison. | **High** if loss construction and tail level are preregistered. | **High.** Dropped halts, stale closes, capped stop losses, later liquidity, and tail winsorization directly contaminate the target. |
| 12 | Survival analysis | **High–medium.** Hazard ratios, survival curves, and time-varying effects are inspectable. Calibration uses survival Brier/log scores and cumulative curves. | **High** if risk sets and time-varying covariates are generated by Replay Clock. | **High.** Using eventual event status before the origin, immortal-time bias, and informative censoring are central; delistings cannot disappear. |
| 13 | Competing-risk models | **High–medium.** Cause-specific hazards and cumulative incidence are distinct and interpretable. Calibrate each cumulative incidence jointly. | **High** with PIT risk sets and explicit first-event semantics. | **High.** Treating competing events as ordinary censoring, learning revised event causes, or omitting bankrupt/delisted outcomes biases probabilities. |
| 14 | Historical analog methods | **High.** Every neighbor and distance contribution can be shown; empirical distribution calibration is direct but noisy. | **High** if search index contains only records available at the origin and the metric is frozen. | **High.** Full-sample scaling/metric learning, later metadata, duplicate event descendants, and survivor-only libraries create deceptively close analogs. |
| 15 | Transparent graph diffusion | **High.** Edge, source shock, decay, and propagated contribution are explicit. Output still needs probability/distribution calibration. | **Medium–high.** Requires versioned edges with valid intervals and PIT source evidence. | **High.** Backfilled relationships, later revenue exposures, stale edges, and graph built from current survivors are direct lookahead/survivorship channels. |
| 16 | Hawkes models | **Medium.** Base and excitation intensities/decays are inspectable, but multivariate kernels become hard to reason about. Time-rescaling and held-out likelihood test calibration. | **Medium–high.** Event clock aligns naturally with replay, provided observation windows/feed gaps are exact. | **High.** Timestamp revisions, bundled news, dedupe learned from future text, market-hours truncation, and missing delisted entities bias intensity. |
| 17 | GNNs | **Low–medium.** Attention/attribution is not an economic explanation. Probability/distribution outputs need independent calibration. | **Medium.** Dynamic snapshots and training cutoffs are possible but difficult to certify. | **Very high.** Current graphs, embeddings pretrained on later text, transductive message passing, target-edge contamination, and survivor node sets can all leak. |
| 18 | FinBERT / financial encoders | **Medium.** Token/span examples can be audited; embedding dimensions are opaque. Calibrate each task and event class, not “sentiment” globally. | **Medium for historical use; high prospectively.** Checkpoint training cutoff/corpus provenance may be unknown. Historical outputs are admissible only with time-locked weights or as exploratory. | **Very high historically.** Pretraining may contain later filings/outcomes; later transcript versions and analyst labels can leak. Prospective frozen inference sharply reduces this risk. |
| 19 | Retrieval-grounded LLM extraction | **Medium–high at claim level** when every field cites exact frozen spans; reasoning remains opaque. Calibrate extraction confidence against adjudicated fields. | **High prospectively; low for old history with modern weights.** Retrieval can be time-locked, parametric memory usually cannot. | **Very high historically.** Modern weights may know outcomes; search indexes, snippets, and document metadata may expose later facts. Such runs are `EXPLORATORY_NON_ADMISSIBLE`. |
| 20 | Autonomous research agents | **Low at system level; medium at artifact level** if tools, evidence, alternatives, and abstentions are fully logged. Confidence prose is not calibration. | **High prospectively** with frozen packets; **unacceptable retrospectively** with outcome-contaminated weights absent a validated blindfold. | **Extreme.** Search engines, model memory, tool retries, mutable prompts, hidden vendor ranking, and self-revision can leak or erase the research path. |
| 21 | Time-series foundation models | **Low.** Zero-shot forecasts have limited economic explanation. Distribution outputs need local calibration. | **Medium–low.** Exact checkpoint and pretraining series/cutoffs are often incompletely disclosed. | **Very high.** Benchmark/pretraining overlap, revised economic series, full-history normalization, and current-only ticker panels can contaminate evaluation. |
| 22 | Deep temporal models | **Low–medium.** Gating/attention is not causal, though feature/horizon ablations help. Probabilistic heads permit PIT/CRPS and conformal checks. | **Medium–high** with strict causal masks and Replay Clock; certification burden is substantial. | **Very high.** Window alignment, centered transforms, label overlap, batch normalization, padding masks, and survivor panels are recurrent failures. |
| 23 | Conformal prediction and calibration | **High at wrapper level.** Interval/set construction is explicit; guarantees depend on assumptions. Evaluate empirical coverage and width by time/event class. | **High** with temporally prior calibration sets and delayed-label control. | **Medium–high.** Random splits violate dependence; calibration reused after tuning leaks; exchangeability failure and selective survivors break naive guarantees. |
| 24 | Online calibration | **High.** Update rule and realized coverage error are explicit. Supports rolling calibration diagnostics and abstention. | **High** because updates use only outcomes revealed before the next prediction. | **Medium–high.** Outcome-delay mistakes, backfilled labels, update-order races, and silent reset/replay divergence create lookahead. |
| 25 | Contextual bandits | **Medium.** Linear/simple bandits expose action values; exploration logic is clear. Reward calibration is not probability calibration. | **Medium** for prospective simulation; historical evaluation is credible only with logged propensities/support. | **Very high.** Deterministic historical policy, selective labels, changing eligible actions, and future-tuned rewards make off-policy estimates unreliable. |
| 26 | Supervised policy learning | **Medium–high** for constrained trees/linear policies; action-specific predictions remain auditable. Calibration must cover outcomes and policy value. | **Medium–high** with temporal cross-fitting, frozen utility, and explicit behavior policy. | **High.** Confounding by the historical selection policy, positivity failures, and post-action variables can manufacture policy gains. |
| 27 | Offline reinforcement learning | **Low.** Value networks and long-horizon credit are difficult to audit. Off-policy confidence is fragile under distribution shift. | **Low–medium.** Replay is possible, but historical action coverage and Markov state are not. | **Extreme.** Extrapolation to unsupported actions, reward leakage, terminal-price revisions, simulator exploitation, and survivor trajectories can dominate apparent value. |
| 28 | Causal forests | **Medium.** Heterogeneity can be summarized, but individual leaf stories are unstable. Honest splitting and CATE calibration/R-loss are required. | **Medium–high** with treatment and confounders fixed before event/action. | **High.** Unmeasured confounding, post-treatment controls, weak overlap, and event selection invalidate causal language; survivor samples change treatment support. |
| 29 | Double machine learning | **High for the target estimand, medium for nuisance models.** Orthogonal score and confidence interval are explicit. | **High** with temporal/cluster cross-fitting and pre-specified estimand. | **High.** Cross-fitting is not protection from future features, bad instruments, unmeasured confounding, overlapping labels, or survivor conditioning. |
| 30 | Matched-event / causal-event-study | **High.** Treated event, controls, abnormal-return window, and match distances are visible. Placebos/pretrends quantify plausibility. | **High** if matches use only pre-event variables and historically eligible controls. | **High.** Post-event matching, current index membership, omitted delistings, event-date snooping, concurrent events, and repeated specification search are core risks. |

## Evidence matrix C — operational burden, failure modes, and mandatory baselines

| # | Model family | Compute, latency, and maintenance | Known failure modes | Strongest appropriate simple baseline |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regularized linear regression | **Low / sub-ms to ms / low.** CPU fit and deterministic scoring; maintain feature scaling, penalty, and coefficient drift reports. | Nonlinearity, unstable correlated-feature selection, outlier sensitivity, mean-only target, false sparsity, regime drift. | Unconditional/event-class historical mean and single-feature OLS with the same costs/universe. |
| 2 | Logistic regression | **Low / sub-ms to ms / low.** Penalized solvers are routine. | Separation, rare-event bias, nonlinear log-odds, bad thresholds, poor calibration after resampling, label ambiguity. | Event-class base rate and one-feature penalized logistic model. |
| 3 | GAMs | **Low–medium / ms / medium.** Spline/boosted shapes need support and drift monitoring. | Over-smoothing, under-smoothing, unmodeled interactions, extrapolation, concurvity, unstable boundary shapes. | Regularized linear/logistic model using identical features. |
| 4 | Hierarchical models | **Medium–high offline / ms–tens of ms / medium–high.** MCMC may be slow; variational/Laplace approximations require accuracy checks. | Prior domination in thin cells, bad pooling taxonomy, centered/noncentered pathologies, nonconvergence, false exchangeability. | Separate pooled and unpooled regularized models; empirical-Bayes shrinkage. |
| 5 | Empirical-Bayes partial pooling | **Low–medium / sub-ms to ms / medium.** Hyperparameter and cell-population monitoring required. | Understated hyperparameter uncertainty, wrong exchangeability pool, overshrinkage of real outliers, target drift. | Raw cell rate/mean with minimum-count rule plus global pooled estimate. |
| 6 | Gradient-boosted trees | **Medium / ms / medium–high.** CPU training is practical; hyperparameter/search ledger and drift tests add burden. | Overfit interactions, split instability, shortcut features, extreme extrapolation, probability overconfidence, brittle feature availability. | Regularized linear/logistic and GAM; require incremental value over both. |
| 7 | Smooth boosted trees | **Medium / ms / medium–high.** Less commodity tooling than GBT/GAM; monitor smoothness and interaction budget. | Too-smooth genuine thresholds, optimization sensitivity, false interpretability, sparse-region artifacts. | GAM/GA2M or shallow constrained GBT. |
| 8 | Learning-to-rank | **Medium / ms / medium–high.** Must build and audit dated lists; pair/list weights are part of model identity. | Rank score lacks cardinal meaning, list composition shift, pair explosion, top-tail instability, optimizing a surrogate disconnected from economics. | Sort a calibrated regularized utility estimate; compare equal candidate lists. |

### Quality rubric

| Code | Evidence class | How this review uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Peer-reviewed journal article in statistics, econometrics, finance, forecasting, or adjacent methods | Highest-weight method/economic-mechanism evidence. Still not proof of Argus alpha or portability. |
| Q2 | Peer-reviewed major conference proceedings | Strong method evidence, especially for ML/NLP. Conference benchmarks are not executable finance evidence. |
| Q3 | Scholarly monograph, official university manuscript, or authoritative survey/review | Used for established definitions, implementation cautions, or synthesis; not treated as an Argus performance test. |
| Q4 | Strong working paper or preprint, clearly **not peer reviewed** in the cited form | Hypothesis-generating only. Cannot support promotion or historical admissibility on its own. |
